Congratulations on your admission into the premier, first and best university in Nigeria — the University of Ibadan (UI).

If you’re reading this before resumption or early into your first semester, and you’re completely new to the university system, this write-up is for you.

#### Session and Semester

An academic session contains two semesters. Each semester lasts about 15 weeks, so you’ll spend roughly 30 weeks in 100 level.

A semester is structured into:

-   11 weeks of lectures and continuous assessment,
-   2 weeks of exam preparation,
-   2 weeks of examinations.

In simple terms:  
You’ll attend classes for 11 weeks, write your tests during those weeks, get 2 weeks to prepare for exams, then write the exams. After that, you may go home or remain on campus — it’s your choice.

#### MTH101 & MTH102

If you’re a science student, you’ll take general introductory courses such as Physics, Chemistry, Biology, Mathematics, and their practicals.

The newly introduced CCMAS curriculum requires **all science students** to take Mathematics, regardless of their department. The number of Math courses varies depending on your program.

-   **Medicine, Physiology, and Dentistry:**MTH101 (Algebra & Trigonometry) and MTH102 (Calculus).
-   **Nursing:**Only MTH101.
-   **Engineering students:**Generally take both MTH101 and MTH102.

Additionally, you’ll take **four GES (General Studies) courses** — two per semester. They include:

-   Introduction to French
-   Reproductive Health
-   Use of English
-   Nigeria and its Culture

GES courses aren’t necessarily difficult, but don’t treat them lightly — they can affect your CGPA, especially if you aim for a strong first-year result.

#### The 11 Weeks

During the 11 lecture weeks, your timetable determines your daily routine. Tests for all your courses will also be conducted within these same weeks. They come quickly — stay alert.

#### Slides

You may be used to relying on textbooks, but in UI, your **lecturer’s slides** should be your primary study material. Supplement with recommended textbooks only when necessary. Most tests and exam questions come directly from the slides

#### **Time**

This is where things get tricky. Campus time moves unusually fast — the 11 weeks can feel like 2 weeks in real life. Before you know it, you’re already standing in front of Hall A at DLC writing exams.

A semester is short. Value your time.

#### **Information**

Almost everything in UI is communicated **online** — test dates, assignments, slides, registration updates, exam timetables, and more.

Avoid staying offline for long. Many students miss tests simply because they didn’t see an announcement. Stay active on your department’s group chat and stay informed.

#### **Obligations**

Why do people go to church? To worship.  
Why do you enter a salon? To make your hair.  
So why are you coming to UI?

The answer seems obvious, but the campus environment can distract you if you’re not careful.

UI offers lecture rooms, reading halls, and CBT centres — but it also has churches, mosques, restaurants, malls, pools, and an entire bustling Ibadan city surrounding it. You’ll receive countless flyers, invitations, and event notices.

Remember: **None of these things are your obligation as a UI student.**

During matriculation, you commit to:

-   Attend lectures
-   Study
-   Do your tests
-   Write your exams
-   Obey school rules
-   Avoid exam malpractice, cultism, vandalism, and other violations

Everything else is optional. Engage in them as you choose — but never forget why you came.
